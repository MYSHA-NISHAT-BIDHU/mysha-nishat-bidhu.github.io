01 / Research perspective

Dependability meets adaptation.

Dynamic computing environments cannot rely on workload estimates alone. Critical events, uncertain demand and limited resources require schedulers that make deliberate trade-offs without obscuring the consequences for time-sensitive tasks.

My research approaches this challenge through scheduler design, predictive modelling and controlled software-simulation studies. The longer-term direction is uncertainty-aware and resource-efficient computing for edge and cyber-physical systems.

AI-DASH

Prediction-informed scheduling using ARIMA and LSTM workload forecasts. The ongoing work explores whether forecast signals improve scheduling decisions across chronologically evaluated workload scenarios.

Manuscript in preparation; not published.

Next question: When should a real-time scheduler trust a prediction?

My proposed future direction examines how uncertainty estimates, critical-service constraints and fallback strategies could be combined in adaptive, resource-constrained computing. This is a prospective research direction, not a claim of completed validation.
